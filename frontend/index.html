        Why rules like "one capital, one number, one symbol" are not enough

        Password123! satisfies every composition rule and is still one of the first patterns tried. Strength comes from length, unpredictability, avoiding common passwords and avoiding personal details.

        10 password security rules

        1. Use a unique password for every account. One breach should not open the rest.
        2. Prefer length. Long passwords and passphrases beat short complex ones.
        3. Avoid predictable information. Names, birthdays, pets, teams and places.
        4. Avoid common passwords. They are guessed first.
        5. Never reuse passwords. Credential stuffing replays leaked logins on other sites.
        6. Use a password manager. It creates and remembers unique passwords for you.
        7. Turn on MFA. A stolen password alone is then not enough.
        8. Never share passwords. No genuine support team asks for yours.
        9. Watch for phishing. Check the address before you type. A fake page defeats any strong password.
        10. Change a password when compromise is suspected or confirmed. Routine forced changes without a reason are not recommended.

        Storing passwords (for developers)

        Sites should never store plaintext passwords. They store a salted hash made with a slow password-hashing function such as Argon2id, bcrypt, scrypt or PBKDF2. Hashing is one-way verification; encryption is reversible. A fast general-purpose hash alone is not suitable for passwords.
